3. Grammar – Hedging

C2 speakers don't always make absolute statements.

Too absolute ❌

> Social media causes depression.



Better ✅

> Social media may contribute to certain mental-health challenges.



Strong but careful:

> The evidence appears to suggest that social media can negatively affect well-being.



Useful hedging expressions:

It could be argued that...

It appears that...

The evidence suggests that...

It is reasonable to assume that...

To some extent...

There is growing evidence that...

This may indicate that...

It would seem that...


🔥 C2 skill: You can make an argument without pretending that every claim is 100% certain.


---

4. Grammar – Concession

Concession means accepting one point before presenting your stronger argument.

Structure:

Although X, Y.

> Although technology has created numerous opportunities, it has also introduced significant risks.



Advanced:

> Much as technology has improved communication, it has also contributed to information overload.



> While it is true that AI increases efficiency, its long-term consequences remain uncertain.



> Granted that automation can reduce costs, it may also displace certain workers.

5. Sentence Transformation

Make these sentences more C2-level.

1.

> Technology is useful, but it has some problems.



2.

> Social media is popular because people like communicating online.



3.

> AI can save time, but it might cause unemployment.



4.

> The company made a lot of money, so it expanded.



5.

> Education is important, but not everyone gets equal opportunities.



Try using:

nevertheless • given that • thereby • albeit • notwithstanding

6. Reading Practice 📖

Read carefully:

> Although artificial intelligence has the potential to enhance productivity across numerous sectors, its widespread adoption raises several complex questions. The displacement of certain occupations, the concentration of technological power, and concerns regarding algorithmic bias cannot simply be dismissed as temporary obstacles. Nevertheless, it would be equally misguided to reject technological advancement altogether. A more pragmatic approach would involve establishing appropriate safeguards while simultaneously encouraging responsible innovation.



Questions

1. What benefits of AI are mentioned?


2. What concerns are raised?


3. Why would rejecting technology altogether be problematic?


4. What approach does the writer recommend?


5. Find words meaning:

practical → ______

encourage → ______

unfairness/bias → ______

7. Speaking Challenge 🎤

Topic:

"Should AI replace humans in the workplace?"

Speak for 5 minutes.

Use this structure:

Introduction

> The question of whether AI should replace human workers is becoming increasingly relevant...



Argument FOR

> One compelling argument in favor of automation is...



Counterargument

> Nevertheless, it would be misleading to overlook...



Rebuttal

> While this concern is legitimate, it could be argued that...



Conclusion

> Ultimately, rather than viewing AI as a replacement for human beings...



Try using at least 8 advanced connectors.

8. C2 Writing Challenge ✍️

Write 300 words:

"Will Artificial Intelligence Create More Jobs Than It Destroys?"

Requirements:

✅ 5 advanced vocabulary words
✅ 4 advanced connectors
✅ 2 hedging expressions
✅ 1 concession sentence
✅ Give arguments for both sides
✅ Strong, logical conclusion

Avoid:

❌ I think AI is good.
❌ AI is very useful.
❌ There are many advantages.

Instead:

> It could be argued that the long-term economic benefits of AI may outweigh the disruption caused by automation.

🧠 Day 5 Golden Rule

B1: AI can create jobs.

B2: AI can create new employment opportunities.

C1: AI has the potential to generate entirely new categories of employment.

C2: While AI-driven automation may render certain occupations obsolete, it could simultaneously give rise to novel forms of employment that are difficult to anticipate at present.
